How ranking works
Your question is reduced to keywords (filler words removed). Each source gets two lookups in parallel: a full-text search, and a title lookup that also finds redirects (in Wikipedia, "CPR" is a redirect to "Cardiopulmonary resuscitation", which full-text search alone misses). A few abbreviations are also looked up spelled out (CPR, AED and similar).
Every result from every source is then scored the same way, strongest signal first: an article whose title is exactly your search, query words in the title, query words together as a phrase in the title, a source match for the kind of question ("how to" favors WikiHow), then query words in the snippet. Results whose title and snippet together are missing some of your search words move down, in proportion to how many are missing.
Two adjustments apply only when the search contains a first-aid or emergency term (CPR, tourniquet, burn, choking, hypothermia and so on): medical and practical sources (medical encyclopedias, MedlinePlus, Ready.gov, WikiHow) get a boost, and pages titled as albums, songs, films, bands and the like move down, unless your search mentions that word. Disambiguation pages always move down. Searches for anything else ("lego", a band name) are ranked on the match alone, so no source is favored.
Ties go to the more on-topic title, then the source's own ranking, then source name. Nothing depends on timing, so the same question always gives the same order.
